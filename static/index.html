About & data

Who represents you in Congress?

See how your senators and House member voted, who funds their campaigns, and who is running in 2026.

About & data sources

MyRep is a nonpartisan, open-source prototype. Everything comes from free public records; nothing here is an endorsement. Party is shown as a plain text label.

    Things to know

    • ZIP codes vs. districts. ZIP codes are mapped with Census ZIP Code Tabulation Areas (ZCTAs). A ZIP can cross district lines; when it does we show every possible district and the share of its land area in each. Confirm with your address at house.gov.
    • Votes. We show each member's most recent roll-call votes. “Yea/Aye” is shown as Yea and “Nay/No” as Nay. Procedural votes are included because they are part of the record.
    • Israel-related votes. Israel-related marks a roll call whose bill or resolution title, amendment purpose, vote question, or vote description contains one of these words: Israel, Israeli, Gaza, Hamas, Iron Dome, West Bank, Palestinian, Palestinians, UNRWA, antisemitism, anti-Semitism, anti Semitism, antisemitic, anti-Semitic (whole words, any capitalization; a hyphen or space between words counts the same). Votes on antisemitism are included under this label even when Israel isn't named. Every roll call in the current Congress (2025–26) is checked, and the labeled votes are listed first on each member's card, newest first. It is a plain keyword match on official titles: it does not judge whether a vote is for or against any side, and it can miss votes where the topic isn't named in the title (for example, a broad spending bill). Titles come from the House Clerk, congress.gov, and Voteview.
    • Money. Totals are for the 2025–2026 FEC reporting period. “Top committees/PACs” are direct contributions from political committees (including party committees) reported to the FEC. “Top employers” are employers that individual donors listed; companies themselves cannot give directly to federal candidates.
    • AIPAC-related flags (2025–26 only). Two separate labels, never added together: Received AIPAC PAC money counts money reported by AIPAC's federal PAC (FEC committee C00797670) as going to the candidate's own campaign committees: direct PAC contributions, plus individual donors' contributions that AIPAC PAC collected and forwarded (“earmarked”). The two parts are shown separately in the Campaign money tab, net of refunds. AIPAC-affiliated super PAC spent … supporting/opposing counts independent expenditures (ads, mail, etc.) reported by United Democracy Project (FEC committee C00799031), a super PAC publicly reported as affiliated with AIPAC (the FEC record itself lists no connected organization). This spending is made independently of the campaign and is not money the campaign received. 48-hour/24-hour notices are skipped when the same spending also appears on a regular report. Labels appear only when the FEC reports a nonzero amount. Money AIPAC PAC gave to party committees or leadership PACs, and spending by other pro-Israel groups, is not counted.
    • Weapons-industry flag (2025–26 only). Received weapons-industry PAC money counts direct contributions reported in FEC bulk data (committee-to-candidate file) from the corporate PACs of major U.S. defense contractors to the candidate's campaign. The list is the five largest Defense Department contractors by FY2024 contract obligations (Congressional Research Service, from SAM.gov data), plus other large DoD prime contractors whose main business is weapons systems, military aircraft, warships, munitions, or defense electronics.     • On your ballot (Wisconsin). For Wisconsin ZIP codes, the “On your ballot” section lists the contests the Wisconsin Elections Commission published for the Nov. 3, 2026 general election: governor/lieutenant governor, attorney general, secretary of state, state treasurer, U.S. House, State Senate, State Assembly, county offices (sheriff, clerk of circuit court, coroner, etc.), and school-district, municipal, and statewide referendum questions. Candidates are in the Commission's ballot order with a plain party label. A ZIP often crosses district, county, and school-district lines, so we show every contest the ZIP touches and the share of the ZIP's land area in each (from Census boundaries); your address decides which are yours. Check your exact ballot at myvote.wi.gov. Not covered: write-in candidates, county supervisors and other district-level local seats, and a few municipal courts. This list was saved on Nov. 3, 2026 and is hidden after Nov. 9. Other states link to Vote.org and vote.gov instead.
    • 2026 candidates are everyone registered with the FEC for the seat. The list can include people who lost a primary, withdrew, or never appeared on a ballot. Several states redrew their House maps for 2026, so a 2026 district number may cover a different area than your current district.
